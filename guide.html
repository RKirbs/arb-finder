How to use Arb Finder

Arb Finder compares odds across US sportsbooks and flags games where you can bet every side and make money whichever side wins. It's free, it runs in your browser, and setup takes about three minutes.

Quick start

  1. Get a free API key from the-odds-api.com. It arrives by email. Detailed steps
  2. Open the Finder, paste the key into box 1, and press Save.
  3. Tick the sports you want and only the sportsbooks you have accounts at.
  4. Press Find arbs. Check each price on the sportsbook, then place every leg.

Want to look around first? Open the Finder with sample data. It doesn't need a key.

What arbitrage betting is

Sportsbooks don't always agree on prices. Sometimes one book is generous on one team while a different book is generous on the other. When the gap is big enough, you can bet both sides at different books and be paid more than you staked whichever team wins. That's an arbitrage, or "arb".

A worked example

Celtics at Knicks. FanDuel has the Knicks at +110. BetMGM has the Celtics at +102. You have $100 to bet.

BetBookOddsStakeReturns
Knicks winFanDuel+110$49.03$102.96
Celtics winBetMGM+102$50.97$102.96
Total$100.00+$2.96

One bet loses and the other wins. Either way you get back $102.96 on $100, a 2.96% profit whichever team wins, as long as both bets are accepted at those odds and settled normally.

How the Finder spots it

Every price implies a probability. +110 implies 47.6% and +102 implies 49.5%. A fair market adds up to 100%, and books normally build in their margin so it adds up to about 104–105%. If the best price on each side, taken from different books, adds up to less than 100%, there's an arb:

47.6% + 49.5% = 97.1%  →  profit = 100 ÷ 97.1 − 1 = 2.96%

The Finder does this for every game, every market and every combination of your books, then sizes your stakes so each outcome pays out the same amount. It checks three kinds of market:

  • Moneyline: who wins. Soccer has three outcomes (home, draw, away), so you place three bets.
  • Spread: Team A −2.5 paired with Team B +2.5. Only exact opposite numbers are paired.
  • Total: Over 47.5 paired with Under 47.5. Only the same number is paired.

Realistic arbs are usually 0.5%–3%. That's small per bet, but it doesn't depend on picking winners.

Run your first search

  1. Pick sports (box 2)

    Only sports in season are listed. Start with two or three you know, like NFL, NBA or NHL. Every sport you tick costs credits on each search.

  2. Pick markets (box 3)

    Moneyline, Spread and Total. Each market you tick costs 1 credit per sport, for up to 10 books.

  3. Pick your sportsbooks (box 4). This step matters most.

    Tick only books you have a funded account at and can legally bet at where you are. An arb that needs a book you can't use is worthless. Eight state-licensed books are ticked by default, so untick any you don't use. Books marked paid plan only return odds with a paid Odds API key.

  4. Check the cost and press Find arbs

    Above the button you'll see something like "This search uses about 6 credits." Press Find arbs. Results usually show up within a few seconds, ranked by profit.

Seeing "Nothing right now" is normal. Real arbs are rare and usually last minutes. Try more books, more sports, or a different time of day. Set Min profit to −1% to see the closest lines and get a feel for how the tool works.

Place the bets

The Finder only shows opportunities. It doesn't place bets. Do this for every arb:

  1. Open both (or all three) sportsbooks to the exact game and market.
  2. Check every price. The Finder's data can be several minutes old. If any price has moved against you, work out whether it's still profitable (the Finder can recalculate after a refresh) or skip it.
  3. Check the bet is the same at every book. Same line (−2.5 vs +2.5), same period (full game, not first half), and the same rules for overtime or a draw.
  4. Place the leg most likely to move or be refused first. That's usually the one with the unusually good price. Once it's accepted, place the other leg(s) straight away.
  5. Make sure each bet was accepted at the stake and odds you wanted. Books sometimes cut your maximum stake or change the price on the bet slip.
If one leg gets refused after the other is placed, you have an open bet that isn't covered. Look for the next-best price on the other side (lower Min profit to see near-misses). A small guaranteed loss is often better than an uncovered bet. Don't chase it.

Avoid getting limited (for longer)

Getting limited is the main reason arbing stops working. Nothing makes an account impossible to limit. Books use automated systems that look at how you bet, not just whether you win. But how you bet does change how long an account lasts. Here's what helps, roughly in order of impact.

  1. Put the "normal" leg on an exchange

    In most arbs, one price is out of line (a sportsbook slow to move) and the other is close to the market. Bet the out-of-line leg at the sportsbook and the other leg at an exchange or prediction market: Novig, ProphetX, Kalshi or Polymarket US. Exchanges match you against other bettors, so they generally don't limit winners, and your sportsbooks see half as many arb bets. Tick the exchanges under 4. Your sportsbooks to include them. Their fees come out of your profit.

  2. Mix in small "cover bets" on popular games

    Books flag accounts where every bet has an edge. Regular customers bet the big games: primetime NFL, national-TV NBA, the favorite, the Over. A few of those small bets make your account look like everyone else's.

    The Finder helps with this. Under game-line results, open Cover bets. It lists the cheapest of these bets at each of your books right now, with the expected cost of a $10 bet. It uses the odds you've already loaded, so it costs no credits.

    • Straight bets are the cheapest option. A standard −110/−110 line costs about 45¢ per $10 bet on average.
    • Small parlays look the most "recreational" but cost the most. Books keep a much bigger cut on parlays, especially same-game parlays: often several times what they keep on a straight bet. If you want parlays in the mix, keep them small ($1–$5), 2–3 legs, on the biggest game of the day.
    • Budget them. Cover bets lose money on average. Treat them as a business cost, track them, and don't let them eat your arb profit. There's no magic ratio. The goal is that your betting history doesn't consist only of arbs.

  3. Bet round amounts

    $50, not $48.37. Set Round stakes to $5 or $10. The Finder recalculates the worst-case profit after rounding, so you can see what it costs (usually cents).

  4. Skip thin arbs at books you want to keep

    A 0.4% arb makes 40¢ per $100 but uses up as much account goodwill as a 3% one. Save your soft-book bets for bigger edges, and use Min profit to filter.

  5. Go easy on props and obscure markets

    Player props, small-college games and niche leagues have low limits and get the most scrutiny. Main lines on major sports draw the least attention.

  6. Vary your size and use the account like a customer

    Don't always bet the maximum the book allows. Leave a balance in rather than withdrawing after every win. Taking promos is fine, but accounts that only bet promos get flagged too.

Never do these. They break the books' terms, books can keep your balance, and some are illegal:

  • Betting through accounts in someone else's name, or having friends or family bet for you ("beards")
  • Opening more than one account at the same book
  • Using a VPN or location spoofing to bet from outside a legal state
  • Giving false information during identity checks

When you do get limited

It happens to nearly everyone who arbs, so don't take it personally. Withdraw your balance, untick that book in the Finder, and move on to books and exchanges you haven't leaned on yet. Limited accounts often still get promos and odds boosts, which can still be worth using.

Player prop arbs

Player props (points, rebounds, passing yards, strikeouts…) are where books disagree most. Each game has hundreds of prop lines, prices react to injury and lineup news at different speeds, and fewer bettors correct the mistakes. That means more arbs than on game lines, but they cost more to find and come with extra risks.

How to scan props

  1. At the top of the Finder's left panel, switch from Game lines to Player props.
  2. Pick one sport. Props are scanned one sport at a time.
  3. Pick props. The defaults are the ones books offer most widely (for the NBA: points, rebounds, assists, 3-pointers). Every extra prop costs more.
  4. Pick how many games to scan, starting with the soonest. Most books post props on game day, so games tonight usually have the most lines.
  5. Check the credit estimate and press Find prop arbs.

The Finder only pairs an Over and an Under on the same player, same stat and same number, such as Brunson Over 25.5 points at one book with Brunson Under 25.5 at another. Different numbers (Over 25.5 vs Under 26.5) are never paired, because both bets could lose.

Prop-specific risks

  • The player doesn't play. Most books void the prop, but the rules differ (some require the player to start, others count any playing time). If one leg is voided and the other isn't, you're no longer covered. Check each book's rules.
  • Lower max stakes. Books often cap prop bets at small amounts, or cut your stake on the bet slip.
  • Fastest route to getting limited. Prop arbing at soft books is heavily watched. See Avoid getting limited.
  • Lines move on news. An injury report can move every prop in a game within seconds. Double-check prices right before betting.

Risks and honest truths

An arb's profit is only locked in if every leg is placed at the prices shown and every bet is settled normally. Here's what goes wrong in practice.

Sportsbooks limit arbers
Arbitrage betting is legal, but sportsbooks don't like it. Their terms let them restrict or close accounts, and most will cut your maximum stakes once they spot a pattern of arb bets. This tool can't stop that. Expect it, and treat each account as having a limited lifespan.
Odds move
The prices here are minutes old. If a price changes between your first and second bet, you're left with an open position. Always check before betting.
Voided bets
Books can cancel bets placed at an obvious pricing error ("palpable error"). If one leg is voided and the other stands, you're no longer covered. Edges over 10% get a warning for this reason.
Different rules
Books can settle the same event differently: overtime, postponed or abandoned games, retirements in tennis, and listed pitchers in baseball. Read each book's house rules for the sport.
Exchange fees
Exchanges and prediction markets (Kalshi, Novig, ProphetX, Polymarket) charge fees or commission that aren't included in the % shown.
Taxes
Gambling winnings are taxable income in the US. Keep records of your bets.
Legality
You must be of legal betting age (21+ in most states, 18+ in a few) and physically in a state where the book is licensed. Sweepstakes apps (Fliff, ReBet, Courtside) are banned in some states, and some states dispute prediction-market sports contracts. Arb Finder deliberately leaves out offshore books: they aren't licensed in any US state, and you'd have little protection if one didn't pay.

FAQ

Is arbitrage betting legal?

Yes. Betting on both sides of a game is legal. But sportsbooks are private businesses and can limit or close your account under their terms.

Do I need a paid Odds API membership?

No. The free Starter plan works with the Finder and covers most major US books. Paid plans add Caesars, Fanatics, ReBet and Courtside, plus far more credits.

I never got the API key email.

Check spam, promotions and "updates" folders, and search your inbox for "Odds API". If it still isn't there, sign up again and double-check the email address.

It says my key was rejected.

See Enter your key. Usually the key was copied incompletely, it's too new to be active yet, or the month's credits are used up.

Why don't I see Caesars or Fanatics?

The Odds API only provides them on paid plans. With a paid key, tick them under 4. Your sportsbooks.

How do I avoid getting banned or limited?

You can't prevent it forever, but you can slow it down a lot: put the "normal" leg on an exchange, mix in small cover bets on popular games, bet round amounts, and skip thin arbs at books you value. Full details in Avoid getting limited.

Can it find player prop arbs?

Yes. Switch to Player props at the top of the Finder. They work on the free plan but use credits much faster. See Player prop arbs.

Why are there no arbs?

That's normal most of the time. Arbs are rare and short-lived. More books and more sports mean more chances. Lines tend to differ most when news breaks (injuries, lineups) and as game time approaches.

An arb was there and then disappeared after I refreshed.

A book moved its price. That's why you check prices before betting and place the first leg quickly.

Is my API key safe here?

It's stored only in your browser and sent only to The Odds API. The site has no server and no analytics. Use Forget key on shared computers.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. The whole thing runs in the browser. You'll need to paste your key once on each device.

Does this site make money from me?

No. There are no ads, no affiliate links and no paid tier. It's free.
